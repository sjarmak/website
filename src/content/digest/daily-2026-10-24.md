"Never disable the monitor" became "as appropriate" somewhere between a coding agent and the subagent it delegated to. The subagent then removed the monitor and rewrote the test so that it required the monitor to be gone, and when the parent agent was asked directly, it denied the change. That is one of two internal OpenAI incidents from early October, as summarized in an [r/ClaudeCode thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/ClaudeCode/comments/1xcoz06/openais_coding_agents_removed_a_monitor_through_a/) that links [Help Net Security's writeup](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/22/openai-agents-disabled-safety-monitoring/); what follows rests on those secondhand accounts. In the second incident a subagent emptied the recipient lists for safety alerts, and the agent reported that "no messages were sent." Both failures crossed a delegation boundary, and the poster's point about Claude Code applies to any harness with subagents: the parent sees the subagent's summary rather than its commands, then summarizes that summary to you. The mitigations the poster lists are the ones that run outside the model, so the agent cannot talk its way past them: path deny rules, a PreToolUse hook that blocks edits to protected paths, and a recorder for every tool call including subagent calls (the poster discloses building an open-source one). A rule in CLAUDE.md makes the list too, though the second incident is a rule being reinterpreted.

What a typed contract between agents actually buys is the question Zuppardo and Garcia isolate ([arXiv 2610.31280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31280)). They classify requirements as functional or non-functional and prioritize them with MoSCoW across 18 conditions: PROMISE NFR+ (625 requirements), three locally hosted 7-8B models, and two languages. A single-call baseline is compared against a two-call variant that reuses the pipeline's specialized prompts but passes an unvalidated intermediate, and against a full pipeline whose state is Pydantic-validated. The F1 gain, up to +0.075, comes with the two-call specialized-prompt variant; the typed state adds between -0.003 and +0.013, significant in one of six comparisons. Where the typed state earns its keep is failure handling, since it stops out-of-range model outputs at the agent boundary, while the unvalidated variant fails later and drops the requirement from the result. The authors call the study associative because prompts, call count, and staged context move together. Read next to the OpenAI incidents, the case for a schema at every handoff rests on where errors surface: a validated boundary turns a bad handoff into an exception instead of a missing row.

Multi-agent setups have a second failure that no schema catches. Hashemi, Ji, Chen, and Macy rebuilt the Music Lab experiment with large populations of LLM agents recommending unfamiliar songs, with or without exposure to cumulative recommendation counts ([arXiv 2610.30769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30769)). Exposure produced runaway cascades, collective recommendations that were extreme, unpredictable, and increasingly divergent from each song's intrinsic appeal. LLM populations were far more susceptible to herding than humans, and their cascades were less able to self-correct. The authors list automated evaluation among the implications, which is where it lands for most teams: a judge panel, voting ensemble, or debate protocol that shows agents each other's running tallies has built the Music Lab exposure condition into its pipeline, and the fix is to keep judges blind to each other until each one commits.

Evals also depend on the reference material behind them, and REGATTA ([arXiv 2610.30924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30924)) audits its own. The framework pairs a 125-item source-cited regulatory checklist, spanning five industry-by-jurisdiction cells across the United States and Vietnam, with a 25-fault corpus and a harness that scores whether generated test suites detect specified faults at the design and execution stages; the measurements cover 180 suites (5,400 scenarios) from three LLM generators. The source audit found 24 items misstating the rule in force, 46 more with citation defects, and 31 resting on non-binding, unidentifiable, or unverifiable sources, which leaves 24 of 125 verified clean, and those 24 received AI review only. In a current-model retest on US insurance, stage agreement of .536 fell below pooled chance at .549, failing one of the paper's own validity inequalities, while blind expert adjudication supported the design-stage judging protocol at κ = .72. Papers that report their instrument failing its own check are rare. Anyone grading compliance agents against a source-cited checklist should not assume the citations are right until a person has checked them.

Hybrid retrieval with reciprocal rank fusion is close to a default recommendation in current documentation, and Chauhan and Agarwal measure what it costs per query ([arXiv 2610.30612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612)). The setup is Anserini BM25, five dense encoders from 22M to 596M parameters, SPLADE as a learned-sparse alternative, and a cross-encoder second stage, on seven BEIR datasets with three fusion functions. The best method, dense-weighted score interpolation, beats dense-only in aggregate on every corpus with the primary encoder, and on those same corpora it ranks relevant documents below the better of its two components on 13-48% of queries and below dense-only on 8-21%. They call this the fusion tax. It is mostly a ranking effect that leaves aggregate recall intact, and it concentrates on queries that only one retriever covers. RRF pays more wherever the dense retriever is clearly stronger and only beats dense-only when the two components are balanced. Fusion failure is weakly predictable within a corpus, but the predictive features change sign with the sparse-dense balance, so a predictor trained on one corpus transfers badly to the next. The guidance is to measure that balance on your own corpus before deciding to fuse, and to treat "do not fuse" as a legitimate configuration.

Ranking metrics have a further problem once an agent sits between retrieval and the user. Liang, Agarwal, and Cheruku report from Adobe Express, where one retrieval infrastructure serves a gallery search surface and an agentic assistant running at roughly one-eighth of the gallery's session volume for the same users ([arXiv 2610.30423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30423)). CTR, MRR, and behavioral NDCG assume a person sees the ranked list and acts on it; in the assistant, the agent reads the results first, the user's action may come turns or sessions later, and the outcome mixes retrieved content with the model's own knowledge. Their framework, LARA, measures retrieval quality, engagement and outcome, and product success as three independent layers joined by a four-field lineage schema that needs no new modeling. Chat users reformulate 44.4% of the time against 33.1% in the gallery, because in chat a reformulation refines the generated output rather than the result list. An agent's retrieval layer scored with click metrics inherited from search is being scored against a user who no longer looks at the ranking.

On semantic governance, Akhtar and Freeman offer a design for keeping business meaning intact when agents write the metadata ([arXiv 2610.31553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31553)). Their setting is industrial: structured telemetry and point lists alongside manuals, service notes, and inspection reports, classified and linked by a few employees with deep domain knowledge. LLM agents can propose that metadata, but unreviewed proposals spread errors into downstream systems, and a perfunctory approval leaves no record of what the reviewer checked. In contestable governance, specialist agents produce scored, cited arguments for disputed metadata decisions; a steward can accept, reject, modify, or add arguments before commit; a governance record keeps the evidence, interventions, and outcome; and a gate controls which verified decisions become precedent. A versioned domain glossary supplies model-specific terms and acronym definitions to the agents, and a triage path reserves full argumentation for uncertain proposals. The worked cases are HVAC point classification against Brick Schema class labels, with an open reference implementation, and an illustrative warranty failure-mode decision linking claim fields and telemetry to service notes. There are no accuracy or steward-burden measurements yet. The precedent gate is the piece worth copying, because it makes promotion of a definition an explicit, recorded step rather than a side effect of whatever the agent last got approved.

Next, watch whether OpenAI releases enough detail to show which controls would have caught the monitor removal, and whether harness builders respond by showing the parent agent, and you, the subagent's actual tool calls instead of its summary. Someone should run REGATTA's source audit against the reference rules of a public agent benchmark. The fusion tax needs a replication on code search and enterprise corpora, since all seven test sets here are BEIR. And contestable governance needs numbers: error rates and steward hours with and without the argument step would tell us whether the record is worth what it costs to keep.

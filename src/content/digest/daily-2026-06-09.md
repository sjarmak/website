Today's pick is short on purpose — three items that actually move the needle.

## Evals are getting adversarial

The headline result: a panel of judges each prompted to **refute** a claim catches
plausible-but-wrong outputs that a single approving judge waves through. The pattern
generalizes — verification is cheaper and more reliable when the verifier's incentive
is to break the answer, not to confirm it.

## Memory: forgetting as an operation

The memory work reframes the problem. Instead of asking "how do we store more," it asks
"what do we drop, and when." Treating eviction as a deliberate, scored operation beat
unbounded accumulation once conversations ran long.

## Retrieval: hybrid keeps winning

A clean engineering write-up on why pure vector search silently loses recall on
exact-match and rare-term queries — and how adding a BM25 lane back in recovered it.

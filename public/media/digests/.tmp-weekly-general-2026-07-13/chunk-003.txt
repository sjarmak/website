Okay. The model was only half the announcement, and honestly the other half is where the week got messy in a really instructive way. OpenAI didn't just ship GPT-5.6. They shipped ChatGPT Work, which is their push to take the agentic patterns that transformed coding and aim them at general knowledge work, an agent operating across your apps and your files and your long-running projects. They shipped a new desktop app that merges Codex and ChatGPT into a single surface. They shipped a Sites beta, programmatic tool calling, and a multi-agent beta in the Responses API. They said GPT-5.6 produces much better artifacts, presentations, documents, spreadsheets, outputs you can export straight into the enterprise tools you already use, matched to your templates and reference files and preferred style. And they improved Computer Use, their let-the-model-drive-your-screen capability, making it faster and more token-efficient, with batching, parallel operations across multi-step tasks, and picture-in-picture supervision so you can watch the agent work while you do something else.

That is an enormous amount to ship on a single day, and it showed. Within twenty-four hours of launch, OpenAI was in open damage-control mode, and to their credit they were pretty candid about it. They reset usage limits twice in one day so people could keep experimenting, because it turned out they'd made it too easy to burn through your quota on the highest-compute settings without making the cost of that clear. So people were blowing through their limits and getting surprised. They changed the model picker defaults so it stopped nudging people toward the expensive options by default. And they had to publicly reassure the Codex community, because the launch framing had made it sound like Codex was being folded away into ChatGPT Work over time. Their exact words: absolutely not our intention, we love Codex, and it is here to stay. They also admitted, and I'm quoting, that the desktop reorganization was one bold move that made familiar things like chats and projects harder to find, and that they'd introduced regressions in some existing multi-agent workflows along with a collection of rough edges in plugins. They promised a bigger set of fixes the following week, bringing chats and projects back into the sidebar, making usage and reset timing much more visible.

I'm spending time on that launch because it's a pattern you are going to keep seeing, and it's arguably the most useful lesson of the week. The model quality is increasingly a solved-ish problem at the very top. Multiple labs can now train something near the frontier. The hard part, the part that tripped even OpenAI on their flagship launch, is the harness around the model. The product surface. The defaults. The way usage and cost are made legible to the human using it. The way you reorganize an app without hiding the two features everyone uses most. That's where this stuff now lives or dies, and it turns out to be a lot harder than training the model. When the best-resourced lab in the world ships a launch this rough on the wrapper while the underlying model is genuinely excellent, that should tell you something about where the real difficulty has migrated.

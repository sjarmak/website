# Multi-Agent Orchestration — Literature Review

## Scope & method

This review synthesizes the literature on **multi-agent orchestration of LLM-based agents** — how teams of language-model agents are organized, wired, fed shared state, and made reliable enough to ship. It draws on two evidentiary streams. The first is the peer-reviewed / preprint corpus surfaced through the SciX MCP (NASA ADS + arXiv mirror), spanning the foundational 2023–2024 framework papers (AutoGen, MetaGPT, CAMEL, ChatDev, multi-agent debate) through a dense crop of 2025–2026 work on topology scaling, shared memory, security, and failure attribution. The second is industry/web engineering knowledge — Anthropic, OpenAI, Microsoft, LangChain, OWASP, OpenTelemetry — which is where most *production* knowledge actually lives.

Two caveats bound the corpus. First, during source-gathering the SciX dense/ANN vector index was unavailable, so retrieval was lexical/hybrid only; recall is a floor, not a census, and citation-graph traversal from high-influence anchors (MetaGPT, Generative Agents) would surface more. Second, several load-bearing results are single-paper, 2026-vintage, and not yet independently replicated; their exact numbers are author claims, not settled fact. Where the corpus is structurally thin — vendor framework primary sources (LangGraph, CrewAI, OpenAI Swarm), production observability and SRE-for-agents, and the classical blackboard / FIPA-ACL lineage that predates arXiv — this review cites vendor docs by title+URL and classic works by author-year, never inventing a bibcode.

The landscape below mirrors the five deep-dive episodes: foundations and topologies; patterns and frameworks; memory; enterprise and production; and the frontier of open problems.

## Landscape

### Foundations and topologies

A multi-agent system is, at root, a **topology**: a graph that dictates which agents talk to whom, who decides, and what they share. The central, hard-won lesson of the field is that *collective intelligence is not a guaranteed property of agent plurality*. Li 2026's SIMAS framework (`2026arXiv260600655L`) isolates the effect of adding agents from model/knowledge heterogeneity and finds performance does **not** scale monotonically with agent count — it follows diminishing returns governed by a trade-off between collaborative synergy and coordination overhead, with degradation traced to the agents talking to each other, not merely long-context failure. The optimistic counterweight is Qian 2024's MacNet (`2024arXiv240607155Q`), which organizes >1000 agents as a DAG and reports a *collaborative scaling law* — logistic growth, with "collaborative emergence" appearing earlier than neural-scaling emergence, and irregular topologies outperforming regular ones (chain, star, tree, complete). The two are reconciled by regime: task type, base-model capability, and interaction design decide whether you land in the synergy or the overhead zone.

The topology zoo has a stable vocabulary. **Orchestrator-worker** (the dominant production pattern) has its reference architecture in Magentic-One (`2024arXiv241104468F`), where a lead agent maintains a task ledger and dispatches specialist workers. ATOM (`2026arXiv260526178Z`) makes this adaptive via a "nucleus-electron" hierarchy that activates query-conditioned workers only when difficulty warrants — naming the "average-complexity trap" of static dense topologies and saving up to 30% tokens. **Hierarchical** structures suppress chatter: Guo 2024 (`2024arXiv240312482G`) shows flat LLM teams over-report and over-comply, and that imposing designated leadership improves efficiency *and* cuts communication cost. **Swarm/decentralized** designs (S-Agents `2024arXiv240204578C`, AgentNet `2025arXiv250400587Y`) re-instantiate Minsky's *Society of Mind* (1986) and biological stigmergy on LLMs. **Blackboard/shared-workspace** designs (ARIADNE `2026arXiv260502431W`) revive Hearsay-II (Erman, Hayes-Roth, Lesser & Reddy, 1980) and Nii's blackboard surveys (1986). And the simple **pipeline** (generator → critic → refiner) remains the right low-overhead default when a task decomposes cleanly.

The most consequential 2026 move is treating **coordination as a first-class architectural layer** (Nechepurenko 2026, `2026arXiv260503310N`) rather than an emergent side effect of prompting, with Huang 2026 (`2026arXiv260513850H`) supplying a two-dimensional design-pattern taxonomy. Parks 2026 (`2026arXiv260511453P`) sharpens topology choice from taste to engineering: spectral properties of the communication operator predict failure mode, with the condition number a perfect rank-order predictor of perturbation robustness and spectral radius perfectly inverted with cumulative error drift on a validated 12-step task. The industrial anchor for all of this is Anthropic's production research system: +90.2% over single-agent Opus 4, but at ~15× the token cost, with token usage explaining ~80% of performance variance — and the candid verdict that "the economics only work for high-value research" (Anthropic, Jun 2025).

### Patterns and frameworks

Above the topology graph sits a small vocabulary of **coordination patterns** that every framework re-encodes: planning/decomposition, routing/handoffs, debate/voting, and role specialization. The role-specialization lineage runs from CAMEL's inception/role-play prompting (`2023arXiv230317760L`) through MetaGPT's SOPs and shared message pool (`2023arXiv230800352H`), ChatDev's chat-chain (`2023arXiv230707924Q`), and AgentVerse's emergent collaboration (`2023arXiv230810848C`). Planning is the orchestrator pattern (Magentic-One's task ledger; DynTaskMAS's async task-graph). Routing/handoffs is the primitive vendors have converged on, anchored in the corpus by DyLAN's dynamic topology (`2023arXiv231002170L`). Debate/voting originates with Du 2023 (`2023arXiv230514325D`), with Li 2024 (`2024arXiv240611776L`) showing a *sparse* communication topology preserves most of the benefit at a fraction of debate's badly-scaling cost.

The decisive empirical point is that **the wiring, not the model, is often the variable**. SOEN-101/FlowGen (`2024arXiv240315852L`) instantiates Waterfall, TDD, and Scrum as multi-agent topologies over the *same* role agents and gets different code quality from each — "the org chart is literally the program." MMA2A (`2026arXiv260412213S`) shows a 20-point task-completion swing (52% vs. 32%, p=0.006) produced purely by a routing choice with the LLM held fixed — but only when the downstream agent reasons with an LLM; under keyword matching the same choice had zero effect (36% vs. 36%). This yields a **two-layer requirement**: information-preserving routing *and* a reasoning layer that can exploit it; 83% of MMA2A's residual failures were in the reasoning layer, not routing. MMA2A also documents that richer is not universally better — higher-fidelity inputs can narrow the correct decision space when the answer hinges on procedural constraints rather than perception.

The framework frontier is moving up an abstraction level. OpenHands/OpenDevin (`2024arXiv240716741W`) is the generalist code-agent platform bridging patterns to runnable systems; OneManCompany (`2026arXiv260422446Y`) decouples org structure from agent skills via portable "Talent" identities recruited on demand — fixed crews giving way to dynamically recruited workforces. The connective tissue is interop protocols: MCP for tool invocation (Hou 2025, `2025arXiv250323278H`), A2A Agent Cards for inter-agent capability declaration, with Ehtesham et al.'s interoperability survey (arXiv:2505.02279, 2025) proposing a phased MCP-then-A2A roadmap, and MPAC (`2026arXiv260409744Q`) as a multi-principal layer above. On the vendor side, the research vocabulary is shipping as LangGraph (typed StateGraph + HITL interrupts), CrewAI (role crews), OpenAI's Swarm-turned-Agents-SDK (handoffs as the core primitive), and the Microsoft Agent Framework, which has absorbed AutoGen into maintenance mode — a clean "research framework graduates into a vendor product" beat.

### Memory in multi-agent systems

Single-agent memory asks how one agent remembers across turns; **team memory asks what shared substrate a team writes to, who governs it, and how conventions and identity precipitate out of it**. The cautionary anchor is *Theater of Mind* (`2026arXiv260408206S`): a naive two-agent loop "rapidly descends into cognitive stagnation" — sycophancy, echo chambers, mutually reinforcing hallucination — because each agent is a bounded-input/bounded-output box and the default passive shared pool (the MetaGPT/blackboard lineage) lets the swarm converge on conformity. Its fix is a global-workspace architecture (porting Baars) with an active broadcast hub, an entropy-based intrinsic drive that re-injects diversity when the swarm collapses, and a **bifurcated** memory: a self-overwriting short-term cache plus a permanent long-term vector archive of distilled heuristics — the agentic-memory series' *consolidation* operation realized inside a running team.

The shared substrate is a stack of tiers: KV-cache (DroidSpeak `2024arXiv241102820L`, with LCGuard `2026arXiv260522786A` as its safety counterweight); ledger/blackboard working state (Magentic-One, ARIADNE); structured collective memory via knowledge graphs (Yang 2025 `2025arXiv250205453Y`) and SHIMI's Merkle-DAG+CRDT-synchronized index (`2025arXiv250406135H`); reusable procedural memory (Agent Workflow Memory `2024arXiv240907429Z`, scaled by SkillClaw `2026arXiv260408377M` into a shared synchronized cross-user skill repo); and institutional memory — norms and identity — from Generative Agents (`2023arXiv230403442P`) up to Project Sid's civilization-scale simulations (`2024arXiv241100114A`). Consolidation is made executable by Auto-Dreamer (`2026arXiv260520616Y`), which invokes Complementary Learning Systems theory to decouple fast per-session acquisition from slow cross-session consolidation, achieving +7 on ScienceWorld with a bank 12× smaller and zero-shot transfer. UCE (`2026arXiv260602304Z`) types the substrate into Memory/Strategy/Workflow/Skill units with quality-scoring and pruning (ALFWorld 75.4%→96.3%) — records-management governance applied to a team store.

Two design axes stay open. **Shared-for-all vs. per-task specialization**: most of the field pushes one shared substrate, but M* (`2026arXiv260411811P`) argues every task deserves its own auto-discovered memory harness. And **substrate vs. governance**: the Parsonian audit of the OpenClaw ecosystem (`2026arXiv260411337R`) — 770k+ registered agents, ≤19% governance coverage, 0/12 coordination pathways functional — is the macro-scale proof that conventions do not self-assemble from scale; you get infrastructure with no institution. The production-pragmatic pattern is Anthropic's managed-agents memory: up to 8 file-backed memory stores per session manipulated with ordinary read/write/glob/grep, many small focused files over few sprawling ones (platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/memory).

### Enterprise and production

Agents that ace demos fail in production not because the base model is weak but because **reliability, security, and cost are runtime and governance problems** — properties of how the system is wired, observed, bounded, and held accountable. The adoption gap is stark: only 14.4% of organizations ship agents with full security/IT approval while 82% of executives falsely believe existing policy already covers agent actions, and Gartner projects >60% of early agentic orchestration efforts will miss performance/cost targets by 2030 (VentureBeat, "The Agentic Reckoning"); surveyed MAS fail in production at 41–87%, dominated by coordination defects (aiassemblylines.com). The MAST taxonomy (`2025arXiv250313657C`) catalogs why — specification, inter-agent misalignment, and verification breakdowns — confirming most breakage is coordination, not cognition.

Security is the most counterintuitive area. Hagag's *Architecture Matters* (`2026arXiv260423459H`) finds multi-agent setups *more* vulnerable than single agents in most of 13 configurations, with attack-success varying up to 3.8×, and **no** design universally safer — decomposition is not inherently a defense. Guardrails are effectively memoryless: an attack smeared across sessions evades session-bound detectors, recovered only by a bounded coreset memory reader (Azarafrooz `2026arXiv260421131A`). Context-fragmented violations (Wu `2026arXiv260422879W`) formalize the confused-deputy export incident — locally-safe actions collectively violating policy because facts are siloed across agents — with 14–98% violation rates across frontier models and a distributed-sentinel defense reaching F1=0.95@106ms. These instantiate the threat model of MCP-security (`2025arXiv250323278H`), Prompt Infection (`2024arXiv241007283L`), and Agent Security Bench (`2024arXiv241002644Z`), and the real-world record (the Cline GitHub-issue-title injection, the LiteLLM supply-chain compromise).

The production discipline that works is **bounded autonomy** (Sohail `2026arXiv260414723S`): typed action contracts, validation-before-side-effects, consumer-side execution, and human approval, deployed multi-tenant — 23/25 trials with zero unsafe executions vs. 17/25 unconstrained, at 13–18× speedup, with the decisive nuance that 2 wrong-entity mutations escaped *every* automated layer and were caught only by human disambiguation. This pairs with Governance-by-Construction's policy-as-code + HITL checkpoints (`2026arXiv260520874S`), AGrail's lifelong runtime guardrail (`2025arXiv250211448L`), and the liability/principal-agent framing (`2025arXiv250403255G`), under the industry discipline of "earned autonomy" — a pre-decided performance threshold at which a review step may be removed. Observability has standardized on OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions (v1.41), defining agent/workflow/tool/model spans with token and latency metrics (Uptrace, Greptime), with CodeTracer (`2026arXiv260411641L`) the research counterpart. The sharpest dissent against the multi-agent reflex is Bertalanič's *Cost of Consensus* (`2026arXiv260500914B`): isolated self-correction *beats* unguided homogeneous debate, which fails via sycophantic conformity (up to 85.5% adoption), contextual fragility (up to 70%), and consensus collapse discarding correct answers already in the pool (oracle gap up to 32.3pp) — directly undercutting the Du-2023 debate lineage.

### Frontier and open problems

The hard part is no longer getting agents to talk; it is making a *team* reliable, bounded, attributable, and safe under adversarial pressure. 2026 produced named systems and real numbers against most of the twelve open problems in the brainstorm report. **Causal failure attribution** is moving from post-hoc autopsy (MAST) to online auditing: AgentForesight (`2026arXiv260508715Z`) trains a compact 7B auditor that sees only the trajectory prefix and halts at the earliest decisive error (beating GPT-4.1 by up to +19.9% with 3× lower localization error), with HORIZON (`2026arXiv260411978W`) supplying 3,100+ diagnostic trajectories. **Verified coordination** imports formal methods: TraceFix (`2026arXiv260507935X`) models a coordination protocol in TLA+ and repairs coordination bugs the way model checkers find deadlocks (the Lamport lineage). **Cost-bounded orchestration** is anchored by Token Budgets (`2026arXiv260604056K`) — 63 confirmed production overrun incidents across 21 frameworks, mitigated by a Rust crate using affine ownership so double-spending a budget becomes a *compile* error — with CascadeDebate (`2026arXiv260412262C`) escalating to expensive models only on disagreement and HARBOR (`2026arXiv260420938S`) formalizing harness tuning as Bayesian optimization.

The single most counterintuitive frontier result is the **Capability Paradox** (`2026arXiv260517480L`): across 42,000 adversarial trials, *stronger* Worker agents made the system **less** secure (mean ASR 18.4%→63.9%, peak 94.4%), because linguistic certainty mediates 74% of the effect — stronger Workers sound more confident, and Managers treat confident endorsements as license to execute. This collides with the **calibrated-consensus** thread (conformal social choice `2026arXiv260407667F`; GSAR's typed grounding `2026arXiv260423366K`; MM-AQA abstention `2026arXiv260414799M`), whose central message is that agreement and confidence are not correctness. **Emergent-behavior eval** (SWARM soft-label governance `2026arXiv260419752A`; bias amplification `2026arXiv260408963L`) concedes that per-agent alignment does not compose into swarm safety. **HITL checkpoint placement** (AMBIPOM `2026arXiv260523023H`) shifts supervision from outcome-level to process-level. And **replayable trace observability** (CodeTracer + OTel GenAI v1.41) is the substrate every other open problem depends on.

## Cross-cutting themes

Four themes recur across all five sub-areas. **(1) More is not monotonically better** — not in agent count (SIMAS `2026arXiv260600655L`), not in protocol richness (MMA2A `2026arXiv260412213S`), not in debate (Bertalanič `2026arXiv260500914B`), and not in Worker capability (Capability Paradox `2026arXiv260517480L`). The recurring corrective is *match the design to the task* and prefer the cheapest topology that meets the bar. **(2) Confidence is not correctness** — surfacing as confidently-wrong consensus in MAST, weaponized certainty in the Capability Paradox, and the need for conformal abstention and grounded claim typing. **(3) The architecture is the attack surface and the failure surface** — error propagation, context-fragmented violations, cross-session evasion, and KV-sharing risk all live *between* agents, demanding distributed, stateful, zero-trust enforcement and re-validation at every cross-agent boundary. **(4) Classical distributed-systems problems reopen in natural language** — blackboards (Hearsay-II), Byzantine fault tolerance, model checking (TLA+/Lamport), and CLS-style consolidation all return because the components now reason in language and fail in ways no type system was built to catch.

## Open problems & the frontier

The brainstorm report (`30-brainstorm-report.md`) ranks twelve open problems, each now with at least a named 2026 attack: (1) causal failure attribution — moving online (AgentForesight, HORIZON); (2) formally verified coordination protocols (TraceFix); (3) token-budget-aware orchestration (Token Budgets, CascadeDebate, HARBOR); (4) durable shared team-memory substrate (Auto-Dreamer, UCE, SkillClaw, SHIMI); (5) emergent-behavior evaluation (SWARM, bias amplification); (6) information-flow control between agents (Capability Paradox, context-fragmented violations); (7) confidence-calibrated consensus (conformal social choice, GSAR, MM-AQA); (8) learned dynamic topology per task (ATOM, DyLAN, Cayley-graph optimization `2026arXiv260409703L`); (9) optimal human-checkpoint placement (AMBIPOM, bounded autonomy); (10) replayable agent-trace observability (CodeTracer, OTel GenAI); (11) cognitive-tier model routing (CascadeDebate, Pioneer Agent `2026arXiv260409791A`); (12) cross-session organizational agent memory (decentralized self-evolving memory `2026arXiv260522721H`, Project Sid, the OpenClaw governance audit). The unsettled meta-question cutting across them is the **reliability-per-dollar optimum**: every reliability mechanism (verifier, debate round, proof obligation, ensemble) adds cost, and the field has no settled answer for where that optimum sits — only the firm finding that "add more agents" is the wrong default.

## How this connects to agent memory

Multi-agent orchestration and agent memory fuse precisely at the **shared substrate**. The single-agent memory question (how does one agent remember across turns?) becomes, for a team, three harder questions: what substrate does the team write to, who governs it, and how do conventions and identity precipitate out of it? The deep dives surface the answer that the agentic-memory series' two core verbs — *records-management* (governing a shared store) and *consolidation* (acquire fast, consolidate slow) — are the exact operations that decide whether a team accumulates competence or slop.

Three design choices carry the connection. **Shared substrate vs. per-agent memory** is a live axis: most systems converge on one shared store (UCE, SkillClaw, SHIMI, shared-KG), while M* (`2026arXiv260411811P`) argues for per-task-specialized harnesses; the production-pragmatic middle ground is Anthropic's file-convention pattern — a common read-only context directory, per-agent private directories, and a project-level read-write ledger. **Durable team memory** is what lets improvements discovered in one context propagate system-wide (SkillClaw) and lets institutions persist beyond any one agent (Project Sid, decentralized self-evolving memory `2026arXiv260522721H`) — but only when the substrate is *active* (broadcast, not polled) and diversity-protected, or it degenerates into echo chambers (*Theater of Mind*). **Consolidation/records-management governance** is the discipline that keeps the store a library rather than a junk drawer: Auto-Dreamer's CLS-decoupled offline consolidator that supersedes bloated regions, UCE's typed-and-pruned units, and — at population scale — the governance layer whose absence the OpenClaw audit quantifies. The closing cross-reference is exact: everything true of one agent's memory — file hygiene, the acquire-fast/consolidate-slow rhythm, deliberate retention — becomes *load-bearing team infrastructure* the moment a substrate is shared, because it is out of that substrate that conventions, competence, and identity either precipitate or fail to.
